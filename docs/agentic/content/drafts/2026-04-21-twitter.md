# Tuesday thread — "You don't need to remember the maths"

**1/**
Your child walks in. "Mum, I don't get fractions."

You haven't thought about fractions since JSS1. Maybe earlier. The panic starts climbing.

Here's the part nobody tells parents: you don't need to remember the maths. You need 15 minutes and the right questions. 🧵

**2/**
The problem isn't that parents are bad at helping.

It's that "help with homework" feels like "teach the topic" — and almost none of us were trained as teachers.

But primary-school learning doesn't move because of a parent who knows the answer. It moves because of the conversation.

**3/**
What actually lands for a kid in Lagos, Kumasi, or Nairobi is a parent who knows what to ask:

• "What did teacher say about this?"
• "Show me how you tried it."
• "Where exactly did it stop making sense?"

Three questions. No maths degree required.

**4/**
Try this tonight:

Ask your child to teach YOU the topic. Give them 5 minutes.

If they can explain it back, they've got it. If they stumble at a specific spot — you've just found the 15 minutes that matter most.

That's the whole trick.

**5/**
What a good "how to help" guide looks like, in practice:

• The topic in plain language (no jargon)
• 2–3 examples from market day, kitchen, or the walk home
• Questions to ask, not answers to give
• One 15-minute activity you can actually do

Not a lecture. A map.

**6/**
This is why we built Awade.

You pick your child's grade and the topic they're stuck on. You get a guide shaped for *your* country's curriculum, in under a minute.

It's for the parent on a matatu, in a Keke, between shifts. The one who cares — and has 15 minutes.

**7/**
You're not behind because you don't remember fractions.

You're the person who knows your child best. That's the part a textbook can't do.
